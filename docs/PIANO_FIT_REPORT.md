**The headline is not "do they fit" — they do. It is that a 17.3 jo room,**
**once lined, is volumetrically small for two grand pianos, and that the**
**door is a plausible show-stopper.** Both are cheap to fix on the drawing
board and expensive to fix on site.

## 4. How much clear floor do two grand pianos actually need?

Clearances applied (ASSUMED):

- wall → piano case: **500 mm** (tuning and moving access; a rim hard against a wall is also acoustically poor)
- piano → piano: **600 mm**
- bench + player zone: **1100 mm deep × 1400 mm wide** per instrument

The search is exact: the two case outlines are real polygons (straight bass
side, curved bentside, narrow tail), and the solver minimises the enclosing
room over every relative offset of the two instruments.

The five layouts tested:

- **Nested duo (interlocked, keyboards opposed)** (対向ネスト配置) — The standard two-piano concert layout: tails interlocked, players face each other across the keyboards. Most space-efficient and the only layout with real ensemble sightlines. Normally requires the lid of the inner instrument to be removed or half-propped.
- **Mirrored, opposed, not interlocked** (対向非ネスト配置) — Keyboards opposed but the cases kept clear of one another. Both lids may be fully propped. Costs significantly more floor than nesting.
- **Parallel, same direction** (並列同方向配置) — Both keyboards face the same way. Teaching / two-piano practice layout. No mutual sightline between players.
- **Perpendicular (L)** (L字直交配置) — One instrument rotated a quarter turn. Sometimes the only way into an awkward plan; poor ensemble geometry.
- **Perpendicular (mirrored L)** (L字直交配置(反転)) — As above, opposite hand.

## 6. Ceiling height, volume and modal behaviour

Volume guidance for two grands: **76 m³** — ~38 m3 per grand (practice-room guidance; 30 m3/grand is the floor below which low-frequency modal behaviour dominates).

The lining costs **350 mm** of ceiling height (150 mm floating floor + 200 mm isolated ceiling).

| Gross ceiling (ASSUMED) | Net ceiling | 令21条 ≥2.1 m | Lid-open clearance | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 2.400 m | **2.050 m** | ✗ **BELOW MINIMUM** | 50 mm | **not lawful as a 居室 at this build-up** |
| 2.500 m | **2.150 m** | ✓ | 150 mm | lawful, but the margin is inside the build-up tolerance |
| 2.720 m | **2.370 m** | ✓ | 370 mm | comfortable |
| 3.000 m | **2.650 m** | ✓ | 650 mm | comfortable |

> **建築基準法施行令第21条** requires a 居室 to have a ceiling height of
> 2.1 m or more (<https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325CO0000000338>). A music
> room used continuously falls within 居室 as defined in 建築基準法第2条
> 第4号 (<https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325AC0000000201>). The acoustic
> build-up assumed here consumes 350 mm, so a 2.450 m gross ceiling is the
> minimum that still complies once the room is lined. Confirm against the
> section drawing before the 確認申請. *Regulatory checkpoint for
> coordination, not a legal opinion.*

## 7. Getting the instruments into the room — the overlooked constraint

A grand piano is moved on its bass side on a skid. Legs and pedal lyre come
off; the case itself does not get smaller. What has to pass through the door:

| Instrument | Length on skid | Thickness (rim depth) | Height on skid | Mass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Yamaha C3X | 1.86 m | 0.35 m | 1.64 m | 320 kg |
| Yamaha C7X | 2.27 m | 0.36 m | 1.66 m | 405 kg |
| Yamaha CFX | 2.75 m | 0.38 m | 1.73 m | 500 kg |

Assumed acoustic door clear opening: **750 × 2000 mm**. 
Movers' practical minimum: **800 × 1900 mm**.

> ⚠️ **RISK — DELIVERY.** A typical single-leaf acoustic door (750 mm clear) is **50 mm narrower** than the practical minimum for a grand piano. Acoustic doors are heavy, gasketed and usually *narrower* than an ordinary internal door, which is exactly the wrong direction. This is a sequencing-and-detailing problem that has to be solved on the drawing board, not on delivery day.

Resolution options (none of them free):
- Oversize / double-leaf acoustic door
- Removable acoustic wall panel used once at installation
- Full-height sliding sash (hakidashi-mado) to the garden as the delivery route
- Install both instruments before the acoustic lining is closed up (sequencing constraint on the builder)

Also required and not yet known: the turning geometry from the entrance,
through the hall, into the room opening for a rigid 2.75 m object; and the floating floor's point load capacity under 905 kg of instrument on eight castors.

## 8. What this report does NOT establish

1. **That the room is any of the shapes in §3.** No plan was supplied. The
   real room may be non-rectangular, may have a structural column or a
   duct bulkhead in it, and may have its door and window positions in the
   worst possible place for a piano layout.
2. **That the acoustic build-up is as assumed.** No acoustic specification
   was supplied. At the 200 mm end of the range each plan dimension loses
   400 mm instead of 300 mm.
3. **That the ceiling height is workable.** UNRESOLVED pending the section.
4. **That the structure can carry it.** A floating floor plus two grands is
   a substantial superimposed load; that is the structural engineer's call.

Every one of these is a `UNRESOLVED` entry in `params/house_params.json`.
